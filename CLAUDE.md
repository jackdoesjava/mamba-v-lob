# CLAUDE.md

Context for anyone using Claude Code in this repository. Read this before touching
`paper/main.tex`.

## What this project is

A short research paper, targeted at an arXiv preprint and nothing more ambitious, proving
a closed-form forward-invariant bound on the hidden state of a Mamba-style selective state
space model. The argument: under the exact zero-order hold the state update is a convex
combination of the previous state and an input-dependent equilibrium, so a bound on the
equilibrium is a box the state can never leave, at any sequence length, with no unrolling.
The bound's one premise is discharged by the model's own layer normalisation. The chain
is formalised in Isabelle/HOL. Limit order book forecasting is the demonstration only;
the claim is about the architecture and the certification consumes no data.

Authors: Jack Humphries (Computer Science, Surrey) wrote the model, the interval domain,
the certification pipeline, the experiments and the bulk of the paper. Daniel Midgley
(Mathematics, Surrey) wrote the Isabelle formalisation and is writing the two paper
sections that describe it and relate it to prior work.

## What Section 7 needs to say

Three comparisons. All facts below were checked against the primary sources on
1 September 2026 and can be relied on.

**Jacoby, Barrett and Katz, ATVA 2020** (full version arXiv:2004.02462). Their step is
`v_i^t = f(W_i v_{i-1}^t + H_i \tilde v_i^t + b_i)` with `f = ReLU`; the recurrence enters
through `H` on the memory unit and there is a bias. They search for invariants of an
affine-in-time template `alpha_l (t-1) <= v~^t <= alpha_u (t-1)`, use Marabou as the
feed-forward back-end, and the loop terminates on proof, counterexample, or timeout, in
which case the result is explicitly inconclusive. They state the class limit themselves,
verbatim: "While we focus here on vanilla RNNs, our technique could be extended to, e.g.,
LSTMs or GRUs; we leave this for future work." The contrast worth drawing: their
invariants are permitted to grow with `t` by construction; ours is constant in `t`. That
is a difference in the shape of the certificate, not only in its tightness.

**Tran et al., HSCC 2023.** Star-set reachability, the strongest representative of the
unrolling route: precise, and valid at the length it was computed for.

**Bonassi, Farina and Scattolini, 2021.** GRU stability. The correct contrast is not
"training versus construction", because a GRU's box is structural too (the activation caps
it). It is that a GRU's box is the activation's range, a constant, whereas ours is a
closed form in the trained weights that moves with the model.

The novelty claim in the paper is "we did not find this", not "this does not exist". The
Limitations section already says the literature search was not exhaustive. Keep it that way.

## Writing rules for the paper

These are the first author's standing preferences and they are not negotiable.

- **Plain, precise, a little dry.** Full declarative sentences with explicit subjects: "we
  prove", "the theory covers". No clipped fragments, no bare imperatives in body text.
- **No flourishes, no editorialising, no meta-commentary.** A sentence states a fact and
  stops. Do not tell the reader how to feel about a result, do not explain why a section
  is where it is, do not perform the argument a second time in a caption.
- **Nothing that reads as machine-written.** No "delve", "crucially", "notably", "it is
  worth noting", "serves to", no decorative em-dash pairs, no triadic flourishes, no
  marketing register.
- **Realistic framing.** This is a first paper by two undergraduates going to arXiv. Do not
  name conference venues as a destination. Do not scale claims to what a conference
  submission would need. Overclaiming reads as fake and the first author actively dislikes it.
- **British spelling.** Normalisation, formalised, parametrisation, artefact.
- **Numbers come from artefacts, never from memory.** If a number is not in
  `docs/certification/*.json`, `models/bounds/mamba_certificate.json` or the existing paper
  text, do not write it. Every number currently in the paper has been verified; do not
  change one without re-verifying against its source.
- **Citations** use `\citep{}` with the keys in `paper/references.bib`. Add entries there
  if a new source is genuinely needed; verify every field before adding.

## Things that were wrong once and must not come back

- The Diebold-Mariano sentence used to say no neural model separates from another. That is
  false: the two Mamba arms separate at `p = 0.0017`, with the bounded (certified) arm
  favoured. The body and the figure caption now say so. Do not revert it.
- An earlier draft wrote Jacoby's step as `ReLU(Wv + Ux)`. The correct form is above.
- "The output" in the paper means the whole-network output, `[-5.1242, 4.3374]`. The
  per-layer readout is `y`. Do not conflate them.
- The `95x` is the invariant against the widened fixed point. The "two orders of magnitude"
  is the unrolled bound climbing towards that fixed point from `L = 10` to `L = 5000`.
  They are one fact seen two ways, not two results.
- Timescale notation: the block uses `\Delta_{\min}`, `\Delta_{\max}`; the propagated box
  in the gap section uses `\delta_{\mathrm{lo}}`, `\delta_{\mathrm{hi}}`. Do not introduce a
  third symbol.
